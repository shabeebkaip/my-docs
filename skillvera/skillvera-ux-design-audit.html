Why design decides this

A Ministry judges the product in the first ten seconds — before it ever reads a feature list.

SkillVera has real ambition: direct Ministry of Sports support by June 2027. When that evaluation happens, no one opens the code. They open the app, log in, and browse — and they form a verdict on credibility before you say a word. Right now the app functions, but the surface tells a first-time viewer "early prototype," not "national platform." That gap is entirely fixable, and closing it is exactly the kind of fast, proactive polish you told us you want from a partner.

01 · First impression

Placeholder and test content is live. Real users currently see developer strings like string and internal coordinates in the running app. This is the fastest, highest-impact win available.

02 · Your #1 priority

The analytics surface is empty. You said data, reporting and numbers matter most. Today the statistics panel renders "no data available" in blank white space — the exact area we should make shine.

03 · The good news

The bones are healthy. The flows exist, the map works, the enrolment path is there. Almost every finding is a finishing pass, not a rebuild — which is why a capable team can move fast here.

The finding to fix first

Developer test data is reaching real users in the live app.

This is the single most important item in the report — and the easiest to explain to anyone. Several screens display raw placeholder values that were meant only for testing. To a member it looks broken; to a Ministry evaluator it reads as "not finished." None of it is a deep problem — it's content and default-value cleanup — but it must be gone before any external eyes land on the product.

Critical · Content Integrity

What a real user sees today

Activity titles that read "// en is the required default locale you have to fill", locations named "string", a coach called "string", a map pin whose popup shows raw GPS coordinates, and a video-game controller photo standing in for a sports session. Individually small. Together, they define the first impression.

// en is the required default locale you...

An internal instruction to developers is being shown to end users as an activity title and description. Seen on the home feed, activities list, and detail screen.

Location: "string" · Coach: "string"

Default API values were never replaced. "string" appears as a location and a coach name — a classic sign the record was created for testing, not curated for members.

power — 24.702786229…, 46.680975258…

Raw latitude/longitude in a map popup. Users should see the activity name, sport, and time — never internal coordinates or debug titles like "power".

"en sport 1 //" · "فعالية تجريبية" (test event)

Test events are live in the public feed. Placeholder names and trial records sit alongside anything real, with no visible separation.

[ game-controller.jpg ] → football / karate

Unrelated imagery with no fallback. A gamepad photo is used as the hero image for sports activities. There is no default sport image or image check when a coach uploads nothing suitable.

"Location Should Here"

Placeholder microcopy shipped as real UI. The Arabic home shows an English placeholder where a location should render — untranslated and unfinished.

Onboarding · Log In

Onboarding & Log In

First screen · first impression
  • The primary button looks disabled. "Log In" is rendered in a dull grey-purple that reads as inactive, while secondary links ("Sign Up", "Continue as a Guest") use the bright accent. The main action should be the most confident thing on screen.
  • Copy typo — "Forget Password?" Should read "Forgot your password?". Small, but it is the first piece of English a Ministry reviewer reads.
  • Social logins include X (Twitter). For a Saudi sports platform, Apple + Google + Nafath/phone carry real value; X adds little and dilutes the row.
  • No SkillVera identity. There is no logo or brand mark on the entry screen — the app opens generic. Brand presence here is a trust signal, especially for institutional partners.
  • Structure is right. Labels, show/hide password, and a guest path are all present and sensibly ordered — good foundations to restyle on.

Home Feed

The screen members live in
  • Empty analytics block. "Most popular activity" renders a white box saying "no data available" — dead space on the home screen, and precisely the area you care most about. It should never ship empty; it needs a designed empty state and real numbers.
  • Placeholder content in the hero card. "en sport 1 //", "Location Should Here", and a mismatched image sit in the most prominent card on the app.
  • Large dead vertical space. "No enrolled activities" leaves half the screen blank with no guidance or next step — it reads as broken rather than calm.
  • Accent colour overused. The yellow-green is applied to headings, links, the active tab, and highlighted dates all at once, so nothing stands out. An accent should mean one thing.
  • Low contrast throughout. Mid-purple text on a mid-purple background sits below comfortable legibility — an accessibility and readability concern the Ministry will care about.
Home · Member Feed
Browse · All Activities

All Activities

The catalogue that sells the platform
  • Inconsistent card anatomy. One card is a flat colour block, the next a full-bleed photo, the next an illustration — location, time, and price shift position between them. A catalogue needs one repeatable card template.
  • Test records mixed with content. "string" locations and locale-instruction titles appear right in the browse list, with no way to tell a real activity from a placeholder.
  • Floating star rating is ambiguous. Stars hover over the image at different spots — unclear whether they show a rating or let you set one, and they collide with the photo.
  • Image quality and relevance vary wildly. From a crisp football pitch to a gamepad — no curation standard or aspect-ratio discipline.
  • Currency and number formatting. The price + SAR glyph alignment is inconsistent between cards and reads awkwardly in the RTL layout.

Activity Details

The screen that converts to an enrolment
  • Description is a developer note. The main body text is "// en is the required default locale you have to fill…" — shown to the exact user you're asking to pay and enrol.
  • "Coach: string". Trust on a booking screen depends on a named, credible coach. A placeholder here directly undermines the enrol decision.
  • Hero image unrelated to the sport. A gamepad photo heads a sports session — no default fallback when a real image is missing.
  • Detail hierarchy is flat. Price, gender, time, coach, and location are all equal-weight pills; nothing guides the eye to what matters for the decision.
  • The "Enroll" CTA is done right. Bright, full-width, unmistakably the primary action — this is the standard the whole app should match.
Detail · Enrol
Discover · Map

Map

A genuinely strong feature, undersold
  • Popup exposes debug data. Tapping a pin shows a title "power" and raw coordinates instead of the activity name, sport, time, and price a member needs.
  • Unstyled default map. Google's default POIs (banks, restaurants) clutter the view; the map isn't themed to SkillVera, so activity pins compete with noise.
  • Pin glyph is unbranded. Markers show a rough "kv" mark rather than a clean, sport-aware pin.
  • The capability is a differentiator. Geo-discovery of nearby academies is exactly the kind of feature that impresses — it just needs a polished presentation.

Bilingual & Calendar

A half-translated experience
  • Languages mix on one screen. An Arabic RTL card shows "From Sun To Thu" in English and Latin placeholder text inside it. The app is currently stuck half-translated.
  • Calendar weekdays are hardcoded English. "SUN SAT FRI…" sit above an Arabic screen; days should localise and follow the RTL week order.
  • Highlighted dates have no meaning. Days 13/26/31 are marked with no legend — a user can't tell why. Highlights need a key (e.g. "your sessions").
  • Latin punctuation bleeds into Arabic. Stray "//" and locale tags break the reading flow of RTL text.

The shift

From "working prototype" to "ready for the Ministry."

After the finishing pass

  • 100% curated, real content — a publish gate blocks placeholders
  • A reporting home built around your data priority
  • One clear, confident primary action per screen
  • Fully localised, disciplined RTL throughout
  • A single, repeatable activity-card system
  • Sport-aware imagery with safe default fallbacks
  • Member-friendly map cards, branded pins
  • Consistent SkillVera identity end to end
Bottom line: nothing here requires tearing the app down. The platform already does the hard part — it runs, it enrols, it maps. What it needs is a disciplined design-and-content finishing pass, led by a team that spots these details and fixes them proactively. That is exactly the work that turns a working product into one a Ministry is comfortable standing behind by June 2027 — and it's the kind of partner we intend to be.
