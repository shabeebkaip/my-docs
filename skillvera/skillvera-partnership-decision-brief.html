SkillVera wants equity instead of cash. Should we take it?

They selected the 30-hrs/month plan, then proposed swapping our development fee for a share in the company so they can put the cash into marketing. This brief reads their feasibility study, weighs the offer honestly, and lands on a recommendation.

For: ShabeebRe: SkillVera partnership offerSource: SkillVera Feasibility Study (Sep 2026)Prepared by: CodeOx
The recommendation, up front

Do the 3-month trial first. Don't price equity on paper promises.

The study is well-built and the upside is real if it executes — but the product is still pre-traction (our own audit found nothing runs through the app yet), and every headline number rests on assumptions that haven't been proven in the market. Taking equity today means buying into projections, not results, in a company and team we don't know yet.

Agree

Start the 30-hrs/month engagement for 3 months. It's small, it gets them building, and it pays us while we both evaluate fit.

Then decide

After 3 months, review real signals. If both teams are good and traction is real → negotiate partnership on informed terms. If not → continue on the hours plan.

Protect us

Keep the two things separate. The trial is a normal paid contract. Equity is a future, separate negotiation — never blur them.

What's on the table

The offer, in plain terms

What they proposed

  • They picked the 30 hrs/month plan from our pricing.
  • Instead of paying our dev fee in cash, they'd give CodeOx a share of the company.
  • Rationale: free up that cash for marketing / academy acquisition.
  • They shared a feasibility study to back the upside.

What it really signals

  • They're cash-conscious — they'd rather part with equity than cash. Could mean tight runway, or high conviction. Worth clarifying.
  • They need a tech team — we'd be on the critical path. That's leverage for us, but also responsibility.
  • The ask is vague — "a share" with no %, valuation, vesting or terms. Nothing to accept yet even if we wanted to.

Read it with a cold eye

The assumptions the whole model rests on

Every big number is revenue = academies × registrants × price × 15%. Change any input and it moves fast. These are the levers to pressure-test before believing the projections — most are unproven today.

The product is pre-traction

Our audit found none of their academy activity runs through the app yet. The study projects adoption that hasn't happened — it's a plan, not a track record.

"60 active registrants per academy / month" is an assumption

The entire per-academy commission (SAR 2,362.5) hangs on this. At 30 registrants it halves; the whole model scales linearly with a number nobody has yet observed.

Zero churn is explicitly assumed

The study itself says "Churn = 0%". Real academy drop-off cuts cumulative revenue directly — the headline figures are a best case, not a base case.

Academies must accept a 15% commission

They already have their customers. Paying 15% for bookings is a real behaviour change — the study lists "academy resistance to commission" as a Medium/High risk itself.

30 academies/month, sustained, by field sales

The hard part isn't the app — it's the field ops + marketing engine that signs and activates academies every month. That's exactly where they want to spend the cash, and it's unproven.

Riyadh saturates in ~16 months

Growth then needs Jeddah/Dammam expansion (more cost, more risk). Year 3 plateaus without it.

Single revenue source + funding gap + PDPL

V1 is commission-only; extra revenue is V2 (post-month 8). They need SAR 1.5–2M runway. And children's-data (PDPL) compliance is a real liability.

The equity offer, weighed

Upside vs exposure

Why it could be worth it

  • If it hits Y2/Y3 (net SAR 6M+/yr), a real share dwarfs any dev fee.
  • Aligned incentives — we build a product we own part of, not just bill hours.
  • Vision-2030-aligned sector with genuine, documented demand.
  • We'd be their tech backbone — strong position to negotiate a fair stake.

Why not now

  • Zero traction — we'd be valuing equity on projections, not results.
  • We swap guaranteed cash for illiquid, high-risk shares in a startup we barely know.
  • The real risk (field sales + marketing execution) is theirs — and unproven.
  • Terms undefined: %, valuation, vesting, board, liquidation — "a share" is not an offer.
  • If the relationship sours, we've under-charged for real work delivered.
What we do

The plan: earn the right to partner

Recommendation

Run the 3-month, 30-hrs/month engagement — then decide on partnership with real data.

It's the lowest-risk way to test both directions. They get building and keep their cash for marketing; we get paid while we watch whether the traction and the team are real. Neither side commits to a partnership blind.

If, after 3 months, the signals below are green and both teams click → open a proper partnership negotiation. If not → simply continue on the hours plan (scale up as needed). No downside either way.

Partnership-readiness signals to watch (3 months)

  • Real traction — academies not just signed but transacting through the app; actual commission collected.
  • The 60-registrants & churn assumptions holding up against real usage.
  • Field execution — are they actually hitting ~30 academies/month?
  • Funding — is the SAR 1.5–2M runway secured?
  • Working relationship — communication, decisions, and paying us on time.

If we do partner — structure smartly

  • Hybrid, not pure equity — reduced cash rate + a smaller equity slice, so we're not fully exposed.
  • Vested & milestone-based — equity earned against delivery/traction milestones, not granted upfront.
  • Real valuation — priced on the actual numbers we'll have seen, with clear %, vesting and exit terms.
  • IP & code ownership clarified (currently the Egyptian team's build).
Before any equity conversation

Questions they must answer

  • Exactly what % equity, at what valuation, with what vesting — and any cash component?
  • Current traction: how many academies are live and actually transacting? How much commission collected to date?
  • Is the SAR 1.5–2M runway secured, and from where? Self-funded, or investors?
  • Cap table — other shareholders, obligations, existing investors?
  • Who owns the code/IP today, and does a partnership transfer it?
  • Is the 15% commission validated with signed academies, or still a proposal?
  • Decision rights — board seat, information rights, what say do we get?
One-line answer to Dr. Salma

"We love the ambition and we're in — let's start now on the 30-hrs plan for three months, deliver real work together, and revisit the partnership once we've both seen the traction and how we work as teams. That way the partnership, if we do it, is built on evidence, not just projections."
