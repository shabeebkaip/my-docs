01 · The Brief

Project overview

PhysioTrio has asked us to build a mobile app that digitizes the full physiotherapy patient journey — from discovering a service, to booking an assessment, to following a treatment plan, tracking recovery and staying engaged afterwards. The app spans three audiences: the patient, the therapist & clinic team, and operations administration.

This is not a product designed from scratch. PhysioTrio's core digital platform already exists — a detailed 9-service catalogue, a 6-step booking flow, hospital-system routing, and a live AI chatbot with WhatsApp booking support. The app carries that same service structure, booking logic and integrations forward rather than reinventing them, which shortens the build and keeps the experience consistent with what patients already know. The app serves patients kingdom-wide — clinic-based and home-care — while routing each clinic booking to the correct hospital system — Makkah on Nixpend, Riyadh on Ecare — behind one seamless experience.

02 · Objectives

What this build sets out to achieve

One connected journey
Discover → Assess → Book → Treat → Recover → Maintain, delivered end to end inside a single app.
Extend, don't rebuild
Reuse PhysioTrio's existing booking flow, service catalogue and hospital-system routing rather than starting over.
Native HMS, no redirects
Therapist directory, availability, booking and payment all run inside the app — never a hand-off to the HMS's own screens.
Recovery beyond the visit
A guided exercise player, progress tracking and outcome charts extend the therapist's work into the days between sessions.
Arabic-first, bilingual
Full Arabic and English experience with RTL/LTR layout for the Saudi patient base.
Two systems, one experience
Makkah runs on Nixpend and Riyadh on Ecare — the app routes each booking to the right HMS automatically, invisibly to the patient.

Dependency & risk — confirm before build

Because nothing hands off to an HMS's own UI, every HMS-touching screen is built against the HMS API. Confirm up front that both Nixpend and Ecare expose a therapist/availability API, a booking-write API, and a payment API / gateway-token flow. If any of these isn't available on either system, it is a scope and timeline risk. Should Ecare not expose the required APIs, Code-Ox will deliver a basic version of the Riyadh integration from our side so the app still launches on schedule. The Nixpend side shares its integration approach and adapter layer with the Vitality app.

06 · Experience Direction

Patterns worth adopting

Informed by a hands-on walkthrough of the client-referenced Meena Health app — we take the ideas, not the execution.

  • Map-pin-first home visits — home-visit booking opens on "pin your location" as step one, before date, time or provider — the primary way to set a visit location, not a fallback behind saved addresses.
  • Insurance eligibility with an honest cash fallback — check the moment a payer is selected; if coverage can't be confirmed, offer "continue as cash" rather than dead-ending the booking.
  • Clear clinic-vs-home entry points — keep "book a clinic session" and "book a home visit" as distinct paths rather than one combined search.
  • Telehealth — optional add — a single virtual follow-up option (e.g. reviewing home-exercise progress) could reuse most of the in-clinic booking flow; flagged as an optional addition, not part of v1.
