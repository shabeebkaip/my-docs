Purpose & Scope

The current site (audited 9 July 2026, scored 46/100) is a template-based build that is slow, invisible to search, and loses buyers at the point of purchase. Rather than patch it, this document scopes a full rebuild from scratch on a new brand-led design system suited to what Al Saber actually sells: used sports cars and premium vehicles, not economy inventory.

In scope

  • UI/UX design (wireframes → mockups) with client approval before development starts
  • New visual identity and design system reflecting a premium/sporty dealer, built on Al Saber's existing brand tokens
  • Full rebuild of every page: home, inventory/browse, car detail, purchase/enquiry, brand pages, about, contact
  • Content Management System — staff add, edit, and remove car listings without a developer
  • SEO built in from day one (not retrofitted): metadata, structured data, sitemap, performance
  • SEO Management Module — an admin panel so staff control search metadata without a developer
  • Enquiry notifications — admin panel lead list + email alert on every submission
  • Financing and trust-signal features standard in the premium-used-car category
  • Bilingual Arabic/English, mobile-first

Out of scope

  • Native mobile app (separate pitch document)
  • Ongoing SEO campaign management and content production (separate SEO proposal)
  • Inventory/DMS back-office beyond what the site's admin needs
02

Brand & Visual Direction

Al Saber sells used sports and premium vehicles — the site should feel like a dealer of that inventory, not a generic classifieds template. Direction: dark, engineered, editorial — closer to a manufacturer configurator site than a marketplace listing page.

Why dark

A dark canvas is the correct choice for this inventory specifically: chrome, paint, and headlights photograph better against navy than white. Every premium car configurator (Porsche, Ferrari, Bentley) uses a dark or dark-staged canvas for exactly this reason.

Follow Al Saber's existing brand identity

The rebuild is designed around Al Saber's own logo and identity, not a new palette invented for this document. The logo is white, with the "S" split into red and blue — the site's color system, typography, and component styling are derived from this mark during the design phase (Section 02's Design & Approval Gate), not fixed here. The exact palette, type pairing, and component details are a design-phase deliverable for client review, not a BRD-level decision.

Two registers, one system

Al Saber's inventory spans both a used-Elantra-range buyer and a Ferrari/Rolls-Royce buyer — these are genuinely different emotional needs, and the site should not show both the same way:

VIP inventory (Ferrari, Rolls-Royce, Bentley, Porsche tier)

  • Full-bleed editorial photography, minimal chrome
  • Understated pricing — "Request viewing" over a shouted number
  • Sharp/square edges (0px radius) — engineered feel
  • Concierge contact, not a generic form

Standard inventory (mainstream used cars)

  • Monthly-payment-first pricing, upfront
  • Trust badges front and center (inspection, warranty)
  • Rounded cards (8px radius) — approachable feel
  • WhatsApp/call CTA, fast transactional path

Both registers share the same tokens and components — the site never feels like two sites bolted together, only two moods within one system.

Reference points used for this direction (from the competitive research already done for Al Saber): Porsche's disciplined, named-token design system; Bentley's swatch-driven configurator; Ferrari's cinematic dark staging and role-based navigation; Syarah's monthly-payment-first, trust-badge-heavy approach for standard inventory. Full detail in mobile-app-design-spec.md.

UI/UX Design & Approval Gate

The website is designed before it is built. Development does not start on a page until Al Saber has approved its design.

  1. Wireframes — page layout and content structure for Home, Browse, Car Detail, and Purchase, in both VIP and Standard treatment
  2. Visual design (high-fidelity mockups) — full-color, on-brand screens for the same pages, desktop and mobile
  3. Client review & sign-off — Al Saber approves the mockups in writing before any page is built
  4. Build — development follows the approved design; changes after this point go through a change request, not a silent redesign mid-build
Why this order: a full rebuild on a new brand direction carries real disagreement risk (a Ferrari-tier "understated pricing" page can look wrong to someone expecting a shouted number). Catching that in a mockup costs an afternoon; catching it after build costs a rebuild.
03

Business Objectives

  1. Look like what it sells. A premium/sporty dealer site, not a generic template — first impression matches the inventory.
  2. Be findable. Full SEO built into the rebuild from day one — the current site is structurally invisible to search (empty metadata, no sitemap, zero structured data).
  3. Be fast. Current measured mobile LCP is 57.5 seconds on a 34MB payload. Target: under 3 seconds.
  4. Convert the buyer. The current purchase flow drops the car the buyer selected and interrogates finances before any contact. Fix the flow, add financing and trust signals standard for this category.

Assumptions & Risks

  • Assumption: Al Saber approves the design direction (Section 02) before build begins — a full rebuild cannot proceed on an unapproved visual system.
  • Assumption: inventory data (make, model, year, price, specs, photos) is complete enough per car to populate the new site; gaps in current listings (some cars have thin descriptions) should be filled before or during migration.
  • Risk: current inventory photography includes watermarked warehouse shots — a premium presentation may call for reshoots, which is outside this scope unless separately agreed.
  • Risk: full rebuild means a cutover — a migration/redirect plan from old URLs to new is required so existing search rankings and shared links aren't lost.
