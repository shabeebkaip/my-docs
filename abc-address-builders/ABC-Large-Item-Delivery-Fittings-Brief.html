Delivery & fittings

Getting the big pieces delivered — and fitted.

RedBox handles the small, parcel-sized 70% beautifully. This brief answers the sales team's fair question: what about the large items and the 30% that need fitting? Here's how furniture is delivered in Saudi Arabia, the partners who do it, and our recommendation for ABC.

Prepared for: ABC sales teamBy: CodeOxScope: large-item logistics + installationSep 2026
The concern, restated

RedBox is right for parcels — not for wardrobes

Smart lockers are sized for small boxes. A dining table, cabinet or bed can't go in a locker, and 30% of ABC's range needs professional fitting. So the store needs a second delivery lane for large and fit-required items.

70%

Ready-to-ship

Small woodwork, décor, artifacts, souvenirs, mementos → RedBox lockers or standard courier. Fully automated at checkout. No change needed.

30%

Large & fit-required

Tables, cabinets, custom pieces → big-and-bulky delivery + installation. Scheduled, two-person handling, on-site fitting.

How furniture is sold here

The Saudi furniture playbook

Every furniture retailer separates the sale from delivery & fitting — the latter is a scheduled, often paid, add-on handled by an in-house crew or a specialist partner.

IKEA

Own model

Flat-pack + paid delivery (from ~SR 39; free over ~SR 1,500) + an optional assembly service booked at checkout — a fitting crew completes it on delivery.

Home Centre / SACO

Delivery + install

Large items delivered and assembled by their own/partner teams, scheduled by appointment — installation quoted or bundled per item.

The takeaway

Two lanes, always

Nobody puts a sofa in a parcel network. Large items = scheduled delivery + fitting, priced separately and shown as an option at checkout.

Three ways to run it

Options for ABC's large-item lane

Option A

Outsource to a 3PL

Big-and-bulky partner (Starlinks / Mille / Naqel) delivers & installs.

  • Zero upfront cost — pay per order
  • Covers all cities from day one
  • Scales with volume, no fleet to manage
  • Per-order margin goes to the partner
  • Less control over the fitting experience
Option B

Own delivery + fitting team

The IKEA model — ABC's own van + 2-person fitting crew.

  • Full control & branded experience
  • Better margin at volume
  • Fitting quality owned in-house
  • Fixed cost (vehicle, crew, tools) regardless of orders
  • Only worth it at steady local volume
Recommended
Option C

Hybrid — use ABC's own fitters

ABC is a fit-out / joinery contractor — the fitting skill already exists in-house.

  • ABC's own carpenters handle Riyadh large items, fittings & the Custom-Works installs
  • Low marginal cost — team already on payroll
  • 3PL covers other cities & overflow
Our opinion

Play to ABC's biggest advantage

Recommendation

Hybrid: in-house fitters for Riyadh, a big-and-bulky 3PL everywhere else.

ABC is already a fit-out and joinery business with skilled carpenters — that's the exact team a furniture store needs for large-item delivery and fitting. Using them for Riyadh large items, the 30% fit-required range and the Custom-Works installations keeps quality high and marginal cost low, with no new fleet to fund.

For Jeddah, Dammam and overflow, plug in a big-and-bulky 3PL with installation (start by piloting Starlinks and Mille) — pay per order, no fixed cost. RedBox keeps doing the small 70%. As out-of-Riyadh volume grows, decide then whether to extend the in-house crew or lean on the 3PL.

How it shows up online

What this looks like at checkout

The store simply routes each product to the right lane by its type — the shopper sees the correct option automatically.

Small items (70%)

Parcel delivery

RedBox locker or standard courier, flat/auto rate — exactly as already planned.

Large & fit-required (30%)

Delivery & installation

A "Delivery & Installation" option with a scheduled date. Priced as a flat fee by zone/size, or "quoted after order" for oversized/custom — mirroring how IKEA and Home Centre handle it. Custom-Works pieces stay on the enquiry path with fitting arranged directly.

Nice tie-in

The same in-house fitting crew serves both the Shop (large-item installs) and Custom Works (kitchens, bespoke) — one team, two revenue streams.

Next steps

To firm this up

  1. Confirm ABC's in-house capacity — can the existing fitting crew take on Riyadh store deliveries + installs alongside project work?
  2. Pilot 1–2 big-and-bulky 3PLs (Starlinks, Mille) — get real rates and a test delivery for out-of-Riyadh coverage.
  3. Set the delivery matrix — which product sizes are RedBox vs freight vs in-house install; define delivery/installation fees by zone.
  4. Configure it on the store — shipping profiles by product type, a "Delivery & Installation" option, and "quote after order" for oversized/custom.
