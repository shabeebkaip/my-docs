The opportunity

A new sales channel for an established brand

Address Builders Company is a Riyadh-based events and experiential-marketing group operating since 1999, with a dedicated Wood Art & Furniture division producing handcrafted, live-edge pieces. Today that craft lives only in a portfolio gallery on addressbuildersco.com — there is no way to actually buy online. This project turns that division into a direct-to-consumer store: ready-made pieces sold online, and bespoke commissions routed straight to the team.

Today

Showcase only. A gallery of wood art and furniture with contact forms — no catalogue, cart, pricing, payments or delivery.

The goal

A bilingual store. Browse and buy ready-to-sell pieces online; enquire directly for custom kitchens, event builds and bespoke furniture.

The market

Built for KSA. Local payments, ZATCA e-invoicing and local last-mile delivery (RedBox/Careem) are table stakes, not extras.

What ABC asked for

Requirements, captured

From our discussion, the store has two clear halves — a shop for stocked products and a consultation path for custom work — plus the local commerce essentials to operate in Saudi Arabia.

Bilingual store

Full Arabic + English, with proper RTL for Arabic — content, catalogue and checkout.

Ready-to-sell catalogue

Tables, small woodworks, artifacts, décor, souvenirs and mementos — each with images, description and parameters (size, wood, finish, price).

Custom works — enquiry only

Custom kitchens, event works, and bespoke tables/furniture handled by direct contact, not add-to-cart.

Online payments

A payment gateway supporting local methods (Mada, STC Pay, Apple Pay, cards) and ideally BNPL (Tabby/Tamara).

Delivery integration

Last-mile via local partners — RedBox lockers, Careem home delivery — plus standard courier options.

Logo & brand assets

Provided by ABC. CodeOx applies the brand consistently across the store's design.

Licensing

E-commerce licensing/compliance — to be coordinated with Adlan.

Social media

An individual to be assigned to run social; CodeOx can support content and setup.

The core idea

One store, two journeys

Ready-made and bespoke are different buying decisions, so the store handles them as two clear paths — a self-serve Shop and a guided Custom Works enquiry. Both feed the same brand and the same team.

Track 1 · Shop

Ready-to-sell products

Stocked pieces bought online end to end.

  • Browse by category — tables, woodworks, artifacts, décor, souvenirs, mementos
  • Product page with images, description, parameters (size, wood, finish)
  • Add to cart → checkout → pay (Mada/STC Pay/Apple Pay/cards/BNPL)
  • Choose delivery — RedBox locker or Careem home — with tracking
  • Automatic ZATCA e-invoice
Track 2 · Custom Works

Bespoke commissions

Kitchens, event builds and custom furniture — a lead, not a checkout.

  • Showcase galleries for custom kitchens, event works, bespoke tables/furniture
  • Enquiry form — requirement, dimensions, budget, photo upload
  • One-tap WhatsApp to the team + lead captured for follow-up
  • Optional consultation booking
  • No cart, no online price — the conversation starts the project
The local plumbing

Integrations that make it work in KSA

These are what separate a real Saudi store from a generic template. On Salla most are native; on a custom build we integrate each directly.

Payments

Local-first checkout

Mada, STC Pay, Apple Pay, Visa/Mastercard, and BNPL (Tabby/Tamara). Via Salla-native or a gateway (Moyasar / Tap / HyperPay).

Delivery

RedBox + Careem

RedBox smart-locker network (50+ cities) with Careem locker-to-home; standard couriers (SMSA/Aramex) as fallback. Live tracking to the customer.

Compliance

ZATCA e-invoicing

Fatoora Phase-2 compliant tax invoices on every order, plus VAT (15%) handling.

Messaging

WhatsApp

Custom-works enquiries and order updates through WhatsApp — the channel KSA buyers actually use.

Growth

SEO / AEO + analytics

Bilingual SEO, structured data, GA4, Meta Pixel and Search Console — so the store is findable and measurable from day one.

Trust

Maroof & policies

Maroof registration, bilingual return/refund/shipping policies and secure checkout — the trust signals Saudi shoppers look for.

How we'd sequence it

Implementation phases

A practical path from decision to launch. Timeline firms up once the platform and product data are confirmed.

  1. Discovery & setup — confirm platform, collect ABC's logo/brand assets and product data, agree information architecture, and check licensing (Adlan) status.
  2. Accounts & compliance — set up merchant/payment account, RedBox/Careem & courier accounts, Maroof and ZATCA readiness.
  3. Design — bilingual UI/UX and a custom theme for both the Shop and Custom-Works tracks.
  4. Build & configure — store setup, categories, custom-works enquiry flow, policies.
  5. Catalogue & content — product upload with ABC's images, descriptions and parameters; Arabic/English copy.
  6. Integrations — payments, RedBox/Careem delivery, ZATCA e-invoicing, WhatsApp, analytics.
  7. Test & train — full UAT (a real test order end to end), then hands-on team training.
  8. Launch & support — go-live with hypercare, then ongoing maintenance and growth.
