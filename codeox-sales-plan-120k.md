## 2. Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs)

### AI Solutions (AIKA + AIWA)

**ICP 1 — KSA Systems Integrators and MEP/ICT Contractors (20–150 employees)**
Pain: Vendor price collection is manual, WhatsApp-based, and untracked. Estimators spend 60% of their time chasing prices and rebuilding BOQs. Tender deadlines are missed. Trigger: Active tender pipeline, growth without headcount increase, failed submission due to missing prices.
Deal size: 25,000–40,000 SAR setup + 5,000–8,000 SAR/month retainer.

**ICP 2 — Medical Clinics and Polyclinics (3–10 doctors, 1–3 branches)**
Pain: Appointment no-shows run at 30–40%. Receptionist bandwidth is bottlenecked by repeat booking calls. Patient WhatsApp queries go unanswered after hours. AIKA handles inbound appointment calls and reminders. AIWA handles WhatsApp patient queries and pre-consultation intake.
Trigger: New branch opening, shift to insurance-based billing, complaints about missed calls.
Deal size: 18,000–28,000 SAR setup + 3,500–5,000 SAR/month.

**ICP 3 — Real Estate Agencies (10–50 agents)**
Pain: Leads from Property Finder and Bayut go cold within 2 hours if not called. Agents are with clients. AIKA calls the lead within 60 seconds of form submission, qualifies budget and timeline, and routes hot leads to the agent. AIWA handles WhatsApp inquiries 24/7.
Trigger: Marketing spend scaling with no increase in conversions, agent complaints about lead quality (actually a response speed problem).
Deal size: 20,000–30,000 SAR setup + 4,000–6,000 SAR/month.

## 4. Go-to-Market Channels — Ranked by ROI for KSA

**Rank 1: WhatsApp BD Outreach (highest ROI, KSA-specific)**
KSA business culture is WhatsApp-first. A well-crafted 3-line WhatsApp from a named person (Shabeeb, not an anonymous number) to the right decision-maker gets a 15–25% reply rate — outperforming cold email by 3–5x. The message must be in Arabic or bilingual, reference a specific pain, and ask one question only. Volume: 80–100 messages/week from a verified Business number.

**Rank 2: Referral Network Activation (highest close rate, lowest cost)**
In KSA, a referred lead closes at 55–70% vs. 25–30% for cold. Every closed deal must trigger an immediate ask: "Who else in your network is dealing with the same problem?" WeCare Technology, once signed, becomes a referral channel into the 1,200+ contractor community. One warm introduction from WeCare is worth 30 cold outreach messages.

**Rank 3: LinkedIn Outreach (best for reaching directors/GMs in larger SMEs)**
LinkedIn penetration among KSA business decision-makers has surged since 2023. Target: Operations Managers, IT Managers, GMs, and Owners of 30–200 person companies. A connection request with a sharp one-line note → a 2-message sequence → a 15-minute call request. Volume: 30–40 connection requests/week. Do not pitch in the first message.

**Rank 4: Partner and Reseller Channel (medium-term, Month 2+)**
Accounting firms, business setup consultancies, and ERP consultants are natural referral partners for Odoo. IT equipment resellers (Cisco, HP resellers) in KSA often get asked about software — they have no answer. A simple revenue-share arrangement (10–15% for qualified referral) turns these into passive pipeline generators.

**Rank 5: Event and Expo Presence (low immediate ROI, high brand ROI)**
Saudi Build (for contractors), Saudi Health, Cityscape, and GITEX Riyadh are the key events. Booth cost is high relative to immediate return. Better approach: attend without a booth, collect cards, and follow up immediately with a WhatsApp within 24 hours of meeting. The follow-up is where the value is.

**Rank 6: Cold Email (lowest ROI, use for gov-adjacent and large enterprise)**
Email works when the recipient is too formal for WhatsApp (CFO of a 500-person company, procurement officer in a quasi-government entity). Personalized, short (under 120 words), with a clear subject line. Use for NEOM-adjacent suppliers, Saudi Aramco subcontractors, large healthcare groups.

## 8. Risk Flags

**Risk 1: Single-deal concentration (Critical)**
If WeCare is the only large deal in July and it slips by 2–3 weeks, the month is dead. Hedge: treat WeCare as a bonus, not the plan. Build enough independent pipeline that the month is safe without it. If by July 14 WeCare is not signed, immediately open two more AI targets with the same urgency.

**Risk 2: Odoo sales cycle mismatch (High)**
ERP deals started in July will close in August at the earliest. Do not model an Odoo close in Month 1 unless a deal is already at proposal stage. Odoo is a Month 2+ revenue lever. Starting pipeline now is correct — just do not count on it for July.

**Risk 3: Non-decision-makers consuming sales time (High)**
In KSA SMEs, the IT coordinator or office manager often takes the first call but has no budget authority. Diagnose authority in the discovery call within the first 10 minutes. Ask directly: "When you've brought in a technology partner before, who was the final decision-maker on the investment?" If the answer is not the person in front of you, ask to include them in the next call before investing proposal time.

**Risk 4: Price objection on AI products (Medium)**
Most common pushback: "We can hire a person for less." Counter: "A coordinator costs 4,000–5,500 SAR/month — and takes sick days, Eid leave, and does not work at 11pm when a contractor sends an urgent RFQ. AIKA and AIWA cost 5,000 SAR/month, run 24/7, and never miss a message. That is the math." Do not discount — reframe the comparison category.

**Risk 5: Bandwidth bottleneck (Medium)**
If Shabeeb is both the BD lead and the delivery lead, pipeline activity will drop whenever a project enters a critical delivery phase. Maintain a 3-week outreach calendar that runs semi-autonomously via templates, and bring in a part-time BD resource by Month 2 if close rate validates the investment.

**Risk 6: Summer deal velocity dip (Low but real)**
July–August in KSA can see slower decision-making as senior management takes leave. Mitigate by targeting owner-operated SMEs — the owner is the GM and is always working — rather than large corporates with procurement committees. Decisions deferred in August close fast in September; build that pipeline now.

**Risk 7: Underpricing and scope creep (Medium)**
Website and app deals at the lower end of the pricing range are vulnerable to scope creep that erodes margin below sustainability. Every proposal must include a fixed scope document with a change request clause. Any work outside the SOW is billable at 350–450 SAR/hour. State this clearly before signing — not after the first change request.
