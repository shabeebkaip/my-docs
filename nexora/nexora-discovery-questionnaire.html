Market Research

Six marketplaces already solve pieces of this problem. None solve all of it.

Fiverr is the closest thing most people picture when they hear "marketplace" — but it's a generalist, gig-based product, not an AI-specific, RFP-driven B2B platform. The table below compares the platforms whose mechanics NEXORA is closest to, across the dimensions that actually matter for scoping this build.

The gap NEXORA sits in

No platform researched combines all of: an AI-specific taxonomy, a full RFP → shortlist → compare → contract flow (Upwork's core), escrow-style payment protection, tiered verification rigor (Clutch's), and — notably — nobody researched runs a double-blind mutual rating system. Upwork's reviews are sequential and visible as they're written, not withheld until both sides submit. That mechanism, already scoped into NEXORA's MVP, is a genuine differentiator, not just a nice-to-have.

Every question below references which of these platforms it borrows a pattern from, so the client can approve "give us Clutch's vetting model" or "give us Upwork's escrow" as a concrete, precedented choice rather than an abstract spec.

A · Discovery & Matching Model

How does a company actually find a provider?

The original brief assumes every customer submits a guided request. Fiverr and Clutch prove plenty of buyers would rather browse first.

Q01 · Precedent: Clutch / GoodFirms browse+filter

Should customers be able to browse/search providers directly at MVP, or only submit a guided request?

Guided-only keeps the MVP simple and funnels everyone through AI-assisted intake. Browse+guided (like Clutch) serves buyers who already know what they want.

Can providers apply to open requests, or only respond to invitations NEXORA sends them?

Invite-only keeps quality controlled and matches the "rule-based matching" promise already in the presentation. Open bidding (Upwork-style) increases provider engagement but adds noise and requires more moderation.

Toptal's entire model is "no bidding, no browsing" — a human matcher shortlists 2–3 people. A lightweight version of this could apply to NEXORA's largest or most sensitive requests.

B · Vetting & Verification

Fiverr uses seller levels earned by performance metrics, not vetting depth. Clutch grades "Verified" vs. "Premier Verified" based on how much due diligence was done.

Does money ever touch the platform?

This is the single biggest cost and complexity driver in the entire build — and the original brief already flags it as "not automatically included in the MVP quotation."

E · Reputation & Trust

NEXORA's one genuinely novel mechanism.

Double-blind mutual rating wasn't found in any platform researched. Worth confirming it stays a headline feature, not something quietly simplified during build.

Q17 · No direct precedent found in research

Confirm double-blind mutual rating stays a Core MVP feature, not simplified to sequential/visible reviews (Upwork-style) to save build time?

Every researched platform's reviews are either sequential and visible as written (Upwork), one-directional (Fiverr — only buyers rate sellers), or replaced entirely by curation (Toptal). Double-blind is NEXORA's actual differentiator.

Confirmed — keep in Core MVPOpen to simplifying for MVP speed
