Executive Summary
Code-OX Technologies is pleased to submit this technical proposal in response to NADEC's Request for Proposal for website design, development, and SEO. NADEC is a 45-year-old, Tadawul-listed, vertically integrated dairy, agriculture, and FMCG company — six dairy farms, two processing plants, and a distribution network reaching 38,000+ points nationally. That operational scale is real and well documented on nadec.com today. What our review found is a website that has not yet caught up to it: strong investor-relations content and a genuinely responsive, bilingual framework sit alongside a Core Web Vitals score that fails on both mobile and desktop, a bilingual implementation missing the reciprocal search-indexing signal it needs, and product pages that don't yet carry the nutrition and ingredient detail a modern FMCG catalog needs.

NADEC's own investment in digital maturity — a dedicated VP of Digital & Technology and an ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI-governance certification achieved in 2025 — is already ahead of what the public website demonstrates. We read this RFP as the next, natural step in that maturity curve: closing the gap between NADEC's operational and governance sophistication and its public-facing digital presence, not a ground-up reinvention of a broken asset. This proposal is scoped accordingly — it preserves and builds on what already works (a solid security posture, integrated investor relations, an accessibility toolbar ahead of most peers) while addressing the items that are holding back performance, search visibility, and commercial content depth.

Competitive Positioning
Why This Approach — Closing the Gap Between Operational Maturity and Digital Presence
We benchmarked nadec.com against the two closest Saudi peers (Almarai, SADAFCO) and two regional/global reference points (Baladna, Al Safi Danone) across design execution, bilingual quality, and how each handles the specific challenge of serving investors and consumers from the same domain. The result is a clear, evidence-backed opportunity: no dairy or FMCG site in NADEC's competitive set currently combines all three of the strengths below — which makes this a genuine first-in-market position, not a catch-up redesign.
Baladna-grade investor-relations integration
Baladna (Qatar Exchange) is the only public dairy peer in our benchmark that treats Financial Information and Share Information as ordinary site content — footer-level, next to Recipes and Blog — rather than a walled-off portal. NADEC's current site already has the right instinct (IR lives in the main navigation, not a subdomain); the opportunity is to lift the visual execution to that same standard, avoiding the siloed-subdomain pattern SADAFCO uses today.
Al Safi Danone-grade visual design
Al Safi Danone (the smallest, private player in the peer set) has the strongest pure visual execution benchmarked — full-bleed lifestyle photography, restrained color use, generous whitespace. NADEC does not need to out-spend Almarai's marketing budget to out-design Almarai's website: the bar set by the market leader is genuinely low, and a disciplined, photography-led design system clears it by a wide margin.
A recipe / nutrition hub no direct Saudi competitor has
Almarai, Baladna, and Al Safi Danone all have a dedicated recipes destination; neither Almarai nor SADAFCO — NADEC's two direct Saudi peers — have a nutrition/education content asset like Baladna's blog. Given NADEC's broader category range (dairy, juice, meat, and produce versus single-category peers), this white space is a larger opportunity for NADEC than for any competitor benchmarked.
A Deliberate Design Judgment

NADEC is a familiar, trusted, 45-year-old brand serving mainstream Saudi households and institutional investors on the same domain — not a novelty challenger brand. Our design direction (detailed on the following page) is built around that read: real photography and one purpose-built 3D/immersive moment, not a decorative effects showcase. The full design-direction rationale, including named international reference sites and the durability argument behind this choice, is available as a companion document.

The NADEC Internal Digital-Maturity Signal
NADEC's Digital & Technology division achieved ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI Management System certification in 2025 — an early Saudi adopter of formal AI governance, ahead of any competitor confirmed in our benchmark. Production-line automation is underway in parallel across both dairy and meat operations, and a comprehensive governance-document refresh was completed across 12 of 16 investor documents between February and May 2026. NADEC's internal digital maturity is genuinely advanced; this proposal's core narrative is that the public website should now visibly reflect it.

Design Strategy
Design Direction — "Grounded Premium"
A single, deliberate aesthetic direction, chosen specifically for NADEC's brand equity — a trusted, 45-year-old, publicly listed institution serving both mainstream households and investors — rather than a generic "modern and clean" brief. Every choice below is a direct read of that constraint, benchmarked against both regional dairy peers and award-tier international reference sites (full curated reference gallery available as a companion design-direction document).
Real over synthetic
Actual farm and plant photography/video — drone footage of the dairy farms, real processing-line footage — as the primary visual language, not stock imagery or default synthetic 3D.
One 3D moment, not a 3D site
A single, purpose-built "farm to carton" 3D/scroll hero on the homepage only. Every other page uses photography, CSS parallax, and scroll-reveal — no WebGL required, and no added Core Web Vitals risk beyond that one hero.
Brand palette carried forward
NADEC's blue (#2B338C/#001971) and green (#7FBC42) are already genuinely encoded in the current build — real brand equity, not a legacy accident. The redesign formalizes these as design tokens rather than replacing them.
Typography carries the "premium" feeling
A confident, well-set type system — a warm serif or humanist serif for headlines evoking 1981 heritage, paired with a clean grotesque for UI/body — does more sustained work than animation, at no mobile-performance cost.
Performance-Safe by Design

This direction exists specifically because nadec.com is already failing Core Web Vitals on mobile (LCP 4.8s, Lighthouse Performance 44/100). The single 3D hero is capability-gated: mobile and low-bandwidth devices receive a static art-directed image or a short sub-2MB video loop instead of the WebGL scene — not a downgraded experience, the correct default given mobile is the confirmed weak platform and the primary channel for KSA FMCG shoppers. The 3D bundle is never requested on a device that fails the capability check, and the hero element is never the LCP-blocking element. Full technical guardrails (draw-call budget, off-main-thread shader compilation, compressed assets) are detailed in the companion design-direction document.

What This Direction Explicitly Avoids
  • Not a WebGL playground. 3D is scoped to exactly one homepage hero, never a sitewide pattern.
  • Not a novelty/challenger tone. NADEC is the incumbent, not the disruptor — the visual direction leans into "trusted, established, nourishing," not spectacle-for-its-own-sake.
  • Not a palette change. The redesign formalizes NADEC's existing brand colors as governed tokens; it does not discard real, already-built brand equity in pursuit of a trend.

Technical Architecture
CMS & Technology Stack Rationale
nadec.com currently runs on Drupal 9/10 with a custom theme (nadec_theme), fronted by Cloudflare for CDN, WAF/DDoS mitigation, and edge caching. This is a sound, enterprise-appropriate stack for a public company managing a large content volume — annual reports, board governance documents, a multilingual news archive, and a filterable documents library. Our recommendation is scoped around that reality rather than a stack change for its own sake.
LayerCurrent State & Recommendation
CMS Platform Recommended path: rebuild on the existing Drupal foundation rather than a full platform migration. Drupal's content-modeling flexibility already supports the investor-document library, multilingual workflow, and webform infrastructure NADEC depends on; a theme-layer and content-architecture rebuild carries materially lower migration risk than moving the governance-document library and bilingual content pipeline to a new platform. This also preserves the security header baseline and TLS configuration already correctly in place.
Content Modeling New structured content types for Product (with nutrition/ingredient/SKU fields — currently absent sitewide), Recipe, Investor Document, and Branch/Location — replacing the current mix of unstructured page content and empty-but-scaffolded templates (Recipes, Departments, Initiatives all currently render with no content despite working UI scaffolding).
Front-End Delivery Component-based theme layer rebuilt against a governed design-token system (colors, type scale, spacing) rather than Drupal's default Bootstrap utility classes left unmapped to brand colors, as found in the current build. Image pipeline upgraded to serve WebP/AVIF with responsive srcset through Drupal's existing image-styles system — currently used only for cropping, not format conversion.
CDN & Edge Cloudflare configuration retained; static asset cache headers corrected from the current 4-hour max-age to long-lived immutable caching for content-hashed CSS/JS/image bundles — a trivial-effort, measurable repeat-visit performance gain.
Hosting & Backend Response Time TTFB currently fails on both mobile (1.7s) and desktop (1.2s) — a server/backend response-time issue, scoped jointly with this development workstream rather than treated as an SEO-only fix. Addressed through backend page-generation profiling and origin cache configuration as part of the performance remediation plan (next page).
Domain Consolidation NADEC's digital footprint is currently split across the primary nadec.com, a third-party careers ATS subdomain, an SAP HANA Cloud-hosted Annual Reports property, and at least one Azure-hosted property — with nadec.com.sa separately serving a mismatched SSL certificate. We recommend consolidating investor-document and careers content under the primary domain (or a properly canonicalized subdomain) as part of this engagement, to stop splitting crawl and brand equity across unrelated hostnames.
Scoping Confirmation Needed

This recommendation assumes a Drupal-foundation rebuild. If NADEC's RFP intends a full platform migration to a different CMS, we're glad to scope that alternative — it is a materially larger undertaking given the investor-document library and bilingual content volume, and should be confirmed explicitly before final commercial scoping.

Technical Architecture
Performance Remediation Plan — Tied to Measured Core Web Vitals
Every item below addresses a specific, measured failure from our PageSpeed Insights capture (22 July 2026, real-user Chrome UX Report field data), not a generic performance checklist.
Failing MetricRoot Cause & Remediation
Mobile LCP — 4.8s (fail) Homepage payload of ~7.4MB, driven substantially by three animated GIF counters (~2.9MB combined) and legacy image formats with no responsive srcset. Remediation: rebuild the statistics/counters section as a lightweight CSS/JS count-up animation (near-zero weight, same visual effect); convert all imagery to WebP/AVIF with responsive variants; set an explicit homepage weight budget (<1.5MB initial load) as a build acceptance criterion; ensure the LCP element is a real image or text block with fetchpriority="high" and no lazy-loading.
Desktop CLS — 0.62 (fail) A CLS this severe is almost always caused by late-loading images, embeds, or web fonts shifting content after initial paint. Remediation: reserve explicit layout space (width/height or aspect-ratio) for all images and embeds; preload critical web fonts; audit third-party script load order (GTM/GA4, social embeds, live-chat widget currently in the CSP allow-list) for layout-shift contribution.
TTFB — 1.7s mobile / 1.2s desktop (fail, both) A server/backend response-time issue, not a front-end asset problem — points at hosting, Drupal page-generation, or origin cache configuration. Remediation: profile backend rendering path, implement page-level cache warming for high-traffic templates, and correct static-asset cache headers (currently 4-hour max-age despite content-hashed filenames that support indefinite caching).
Mobile Lighthouse Performance — 44/100 The aggregate of the LCP, TTFB, and payload issues above. Mobile is prioritized first in the remediation sequence, since it is the clear weak platform (44 vs. 67 desktop) and the primary channel for KSA FMCG shoppers.
What Already Passes — Preserved, Not Rebuilt

INP already passes on both platforms (142ms mobile / 47ms desktop) — the design direction's technical guardrails (off-main-thread shader compilation, capped draw calls for the homepage 3D hero) are built specifically to protect this metric, not risk it. loading="lazy" is already applied to 34 of the homepage's 51 images — extended sitewide rather than reworked. Lighthouse SEO is already a strong 92/100 and Best Practices 96/100 on both platforms — the performance workstream is targeted specifically at Core Web Vitals, not basic hygiene the current build already gets right.

Technical Architecture
Bilingual Arabic/English Methodology
NADEC's site is Arabic-first at the root domain (www.nadec.com serves Arabic by default, with English at /en) — a deliberate, defensible choice for a Saudi-headquartered company that we recommend preserving. The methodology below addresses the specific bilingual gaps our review identified, without disturbing what already works.
AreaCurrent State & Our Approach
Language-Default Behavior The bare domain currently always serves Arabic, regardless of browser language, with no Accept-Language detection. We recommend preserving Arabic-default at the root as an intentional, documented decision (valid for a Saudi-headquartered company), while adding a persistent, visible language toggle on every template so English-preferring visitors self-navigate in one click rather than several.
Reciprocal hreflang Annotation The Arabic homepage's <head> currently links to English via hreflang, but English pages do not link back to Arabic — a one-directional gap search engines read as incomplete. Implemented as a template-level fix: reciprocal hreflang="ar"/hreflang="en" plus x-default tags across every paired AR/EN template, generated automatically from the CMS content-translation relationship rather than hand-maintained per page.
RTL Layout — Native, Not Mirrored Every template is designed RTL-first in the wireframe and prototype stage, not built LTR and flipped after the fact. Any scroll-camera or 3D sequence (the homepage hero) is authored direction-agnostic or explicitly validated in both directions — a 3D camera path does not auto-mirror the way 2D layout does under dir="rtl", so this is a dedicated build-phase task, not an assumption. Standard parallax/scroll-reveal techniques mirror cleanly via CSS logical properties.
Typography — Genuinely Paired, Not Substituted A dedicated Arabic display/text typeface is selected and tested alongside the Latin serif/grotesque pairing for the same "heritage warmth + institutional trust" register — not a default system Arabic font applied underneath a bespoke Latin type system, a common and visible quality gap in bilingual Gulf sites.
Content Parity & Sitemap Coverage Spot-checks confirm English content genuinely exists in parallel to Arabic across most sections — a real strength to preserve — but the XML sitemap only formally lists ~37–40 URLs and treats several English pages as secondary rather than first-class entries. The regenerated sitemap (Scope Item 1, deliverable 08) lists both language versions of every page as first-class, correctly cross-annotated entries.
RTL QA — Budgeted as a Formal Line Item Side-by-side rendered comparison of AR vs. EN across every key template (homepage, product, article, investor, form) at 375px/768px/1440px, confirming icons, carousels, and directional UI elements (chevrons, progress bars, back buttons) mirror correctly — extended to explicitly cover the new hero sequence and any new interactive modules. This is consistently underestimated in bilingual rebuilds; we budget it as a distinct QA phase, not an afterthought inside general testing.

#Sub-Item & Methodology
01 Technical SEOImplement reciprocal hreflang across every AR/EN page pair (the single most consequential technical gap identified). Add a Sitemap: directive to robots.txt and rebuild the XML sitemap — the current sitemap declares exactly 37 URLs, and a full 50-page crawl found 49 of 50 live pages are not represented in it at all, meaning discovery today relies almost entirely on internal linking. Roll out sitewide JSON-LD structured data: Organization (homepage), Product (catalog), BreadcrumbList (all templates), FAQPage, and LocalBusiness (branches/contact) — currently zero instances found sitewide. Migrate product URLs from numeric IDs to keyword-bearing slugs. Implement 301 redirects from every legacy /index.php/en/X URL to its clean /en/X equivalent — a live crawl confirmed at least 4 page pairs (meat, about, feed, juice) currently serve 100%-identical content and duplicate title tags at both URL forms simultaneously, splitting ranking signal for the same page. Fix the 4 broken asset references returning 404 found in the same crawl (e.g. vision2.png, message.jpg on the About page) and the one insecure http:// video embed loading on the About page despite the page itself being served over HTTPS. Add a missing SPF record to complement the site's existing (correctly configured) DMARC policy. Consolidate off-domain career/campaign microsites under the primary domain to stop splitting crawl and link equity.
02 On-Page SEOKSA-specific keyword research across dairy/agri/FMCG terms in both Arabic and English, mapped to category and product pages. Add a single, correctly-scoped <h1> to every template — commercial pages (e.g., the dairy category page and product detail pages) currently have none. Rewrite category/product meta titles and descriptions to be unique and commercially targeted, replacing the current pattern of category pages reusing the homepage's corporate boilerplate description verbatim. Expand product page content with nutritional information, ingredients, and usage occasions, cross-linked into the new recipe/nutrition hub. Replace generic, duplicated icon alt text (currently repeated as "Menu Icon" across visually distinct navigation items) with descriptive labels. Build contextual internal linking between category, product, and content pages beyond the existing nav/breadcrumb structure.

03 Performance / Core Web VitalsExecuted jointly with the development workstream (see Technical Architecture, pages 6–7): mobile prioritized first as the confirmed weak platform (LCP 4.8s, Lighthouse Performance 44/100); TTFB remediated at the hosting/backend layer as a server response-time fix, not a client-side one; desktop CLS failure (0.62) diagnosed and fixed at the layout/embed/font level; imagery converted to WebP/AVIF with responsive srcset to reduce LCP-affecting payload.
04 Off-Page Authority BuildingNADEC's Tadawul-listed status already generates naturally authoritative inbound coverage from Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Zawya, and AGBI — a real, currently underexploited asset. We systematize digital PR to pitch existing earned-media moments (the Ministry of Human Resources CSR Social Responsibility Badge, the Al-Jouf Award for the organic olive oil project, "Product of the Year 2025 – KSA" for Nadec Fresh Milk) to consumer and food press, not only the corporate news page. The new recipe/nutrition hub (Scope Item 1) is built to earn links from food bloggers and lifestyle media. We pursue links from KSA retail, grocery, and foodservice industry directories and trade bodies, and consolidate domain authority per the Technical SEO section rather than splitting it across off-domain microsites. A preliminary backlink pull surfaced two risk items to remediate early: a spam/black-hat promotional anchor text appearing 257 times in the referring-link profile, and the majority of the site's strongest inbound links currently pointing to an off-domain Azure microsite rather than nadec.com itself. Both are addressed via a full backlink audit and disavow-file review, and via the domain-consolidation work in the Technical SEO row above. Domain Rating and referring-domain growth are benchmarked against Almarai on a recurring quarterly basis once live licensed-tool data is available.
05 Local SEONAP data and a dedicated branches page with an embedded map already exist — a solid foundation to build on. The current map is a JS-rendered widget that only exposes the HQ record to search crawlers and static analysis; before schema work begins we run a headless-browser (Playwright) pass to fully enumerate the branch/distribution-point network, rather than building on an incomplete list. We confirm current Google Business Profile status directly with NADEC as the first step, then build/optimize a multi-location GBP program covering HQ, processing plants, and branch locations. LocalBusiness/Organization schema is implemented referencing the fully-enumerated branch data now structured in the CMS, converting NADEC's real physical scale — HQ, six dairy farms, two processing plants, and a nationwide branch network feeding 38,000+ distribution points — into search-visible, machine-readable local presence. NAP consistency is enforced across GBP, the website, social profiles, and third-party directories.
06 GEO — Generative Engine OptimizationA sixth pillar beyond NADEC's original five-item brief, included because it directly extends a narrative NADEC is already telling about itself: the same AI-governance maturity reflected in its Digital & Technology function. We add Organization/Person JSON-LD identity schema and a /llms.txt file so AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) can correctly identify and cite NADEC — neither currently exists. Most competing agency bids for this RFP are unlikely to scope this. (Note: NADEC's homepage content references several ISO-related terms; we will confirm with NADEC's team which specific ISO standards are being referenced before finalizing this narrative in client-facing copy, to keep the connection precise rather than overstated.)

Delivery Approach
Team & Working Methodology
This engagement is delivered by a focused, senior-led team spanning strategy, design, development, and SEO — structured to match the multi-audience nature of NADEC's site (consumers, investors, media, and government-adjacent stakeholders) rather than a single generalist workflow.
Single Point of Accountability
Shabeeb K, Head of Operations at Code-OX, is the named senior contact for this engagement — commercial, technical, and delivery questions route through one accountable owner, not a rotating account team.
Evidence-Led, Not Template-Led
Every scope item in this proposal is traceable to a specific, verifiable finding from our independent audit — the same discipline continues through delivery, with Core Web Vitals and SEO metrics measured against the documented baseline at each milestone, not asserted as "improved."
Milestone-Based Client Review
Structured review points at the end of each phase (wireframes, design sign-off, development complete, pre-launch QA) — NADEC's Digital Media team reviews and approves before the next phase begins.
Public-Company-Appropriate Change Control
Content touching investor disclosures, governance documents, or regulated claims (e.g., product/nutrition data) routes through NADEC's own compliance/regulatory sign-off before publication — we build the CMS workflow to support that review step, not bypass it.
Key Assumptions
  • NADEC provides timely access to existing content (product data, investor documents, brand assets) and a named internal reviewer for each milestone.
  • Nutrition, ingredient, and SKU-level product content is authored with input from NADEC's quality/regulatory team — Code-OX provides the CMS structure and content-entry support, not regulatory sign-off on claims.
  • The CMS-foundation decision (rebuild on Drupal vs. full platform migration) and e-commerce scope (full store vs. store-locator vs. B2B portal) are confirmed at kickoff, as both materially affect timeline and effort.
  • Hosting/infrastructure access sufficient to profile and remediate TTFB is made available during the performance-remediation phase.
  • Google Business Profile access and a licensed off-page SEO tool (Ahrefs/SEMrush/Moz) are made available or provisioned at kickoff to finalize the Local and Off-Page SEO baselines.
