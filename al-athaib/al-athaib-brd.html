03

Selling in cartons — returns in pieces

You sell by the carton, but customers sometimes return single pieces. The system keeps count in pieces behind the scenes, so every carton sold and every piece returned leaves the stock exactly right.

  • Each product knows how many pieces its carton holds.
  • Returns are accepted as full cartons or single pieces.
  • Stock is always correct, down to the piece.
04

Van sales, from loading to day close

Your two salesmen sell from the van. Each van carries its own stock in the system, every movement between the warehouse and the van is recorded, and the day ends with a clear closing — what was loaded, sold, returned, and how much cash came in.

Load the van — stock moves from warehouse to van, recorded by name and time
Sell on the route — by carton or piece, cash or on account
Take returns — even single pieces from a carton
Close the day — sold, returned, remaining and cash, all on one report
Settle — leftover stock goes back, cash is matched to sales
  • Each salesman sees only his own van stock and customers.
  • Management sees all vans together on one screen.
  • Ready for more vans as you grow — the setup repeats, nothing changes.
05

Cash sales and the sales screen

Many of your customers pay cash with no invoice. Each cash sale still reduces stock immediately and is recorded, so the day's cash always matches the day's sales. On the sales screen, the salesman sees the day's total sales value at all times — right next to the stock count.

06

Knowing your true cost — mixed containers

Your containers arrive with mixed items, and each item carries a different import tax. The system will spread each item's own import tax into that item's cost, so the cost you see is the real cost — and every margin and price decision is built on it.

  • Vendor prices can change between purchases — cost stays up to date automatically.
  • The monthly warehouse cost is also added into product cost, and can be raised each month as your rent rises.
07

Protecting your profit on every sale

No one should sell at a loss by accident. Every product has a minimum selling price, and the system reacts to the price the salesman enters:

Above minimumSale goes through normally — healthy margin.
Below minimumSale stops and needs a manager's approval.
Below costClear loss warning — you are selling for less than it cost you.
  • Margin is visible on every sale and in reports.
  • For stock close to its expiry date, the system suggests a reduced price based on the margin still available — so near-expiry stock moves without giving it away.
08

Discounts and offers

  • Salesmen can give discounts, within a limit set per person — anything above the limit needs approval.
  • Offers apply automatically — for example, a better price when the customer buys more cartons.
09

Salesman commissions

  • Products are grouped into categories, and each category carries its own commission rate.
  • Each salesman's commission is reported clearly, ready for payroll.
10

Warehouse and stock tracking

  • Full warehouse flow: receiving, storing, picking and transfers between locations.
  • Each bag carries its own barcode — scanned when it arrives, moves and is sold.
  • Expiry dates are recorded on stock, feeding the near-expiry price suggestions.
11

E-invoicing, assets and people

  • ZATCA e-invoicing: Phase 1 is included now. Phase 2 is not required at this stage and can be added later when you need it.
  • Assets: a register of your vans, computers and tablets, each with its owner, purchase cost and value over time.
  • Payroll: staff salaries managed in the same system, with commissions feeding in.
  • Access by role: salesmen, warehouse staff, accounts and management each see only what they need.
  • Training: Code-Ox trains your van salesmen, warehouse staff and accounts team before go-live.
